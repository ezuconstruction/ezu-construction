The federal government’s new National Heat Pump Rebate pays **$2,000**, or **up to $10,000** for households below the median income, toward a whole-home heat pump. Applications open online in early 2027, but the rebate is **retroactive to installations on or after November 1, 2026**. If your furnace is due for replacement this winter, the clock has already started.

## What it pays

| Household | Rebate |
| --- | --- |
| After-tax income above the provincial median | $2,000 |
| After-tax income at or below the median | Up to $10,000 |

For Ontario, the median thresholds by household size are $46,981 for one person, $92,884 for two, $119,850 for three, $146,217 for four and $158,202 for five or more. After-tax income is line 23600 minus line 43500 of your 2025 return, added together for you and a spouse or partner. To claim the higher amount, you’ll give your Social Insurance Number so the Canada Revenue Agency can confirm your income.

## Who qualifies

- You own the home and live in Canada. It doesn’t have to be your primary residence, so a rental property counts.
- The heat pump is on the program’s eligible product list, identified by its AHRI number.
- It was bought from and installed by an HVAC contractor.
- It’s sized to heat the whole home.
- No federal funding has already been paid for a heat pump at that address.
- One rebate per household, even if you own several properties.

It doesn’t matter how you heat today (gas, oil, propane, electric baseboards or wood), and you don’t have to remove your old system or prove it failed. Emergency replacements and planned upgrades both count. There’s **no energy assessment** before or after.

## What to keep from your contractor

You’ll need to submit receipts within six months, and the program is specific about what they must show:

- the name and address of the person who will apply
- the installing company’s name
- the make, model and **AHRI number** of each unit
- how many heat pumps were installed
- the total project cost, including related work such as ductwork and electrical upgrades

Ask your contractor for this on the invoice now. It’s much harder to get months later.

## Stacking it with Ontario’s rebate

In Ontario, the Home Renovation Savings Program also pays up to $12,000 for a heat pump, with no published end date. Read [renovation rebates for Ontario homeowners](/journal/ontario-home-renovation-rebates), and confirm with both programs how they combine for your installation before you rely on both.

In Toronto, the [Home Energy Loan Program](/journal/toronto-home-energy-loan) can also lend you the upfront cost, repaid on your property tax bill.

## Watch for scams

The government warns that salespeople are calling and knocking on doors citing this rebate. Environment and Climate Change Canada doesn’t make marketing calls or visit homes to sell equipment. Start the conversation with a contractor yourself.

## Is a heat pump right for your house?

For homes heated with natural gas, a heat pump doesn’t always lower the heating bill, and an older house may need insulation, air sealing or an electrical service upgrade first. Our guide to [heat pumps in older GTA homes](/journal/heat-pumps-older-gta-homes) covers what to check.
