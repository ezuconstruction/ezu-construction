A legal basement suite or garden suite can pay for a good share of itself in rent, but it has to be built first, and a legal basement suite in a typical detached house usually costs $120,000 to $200,000. Since January 2025 there’s been a federal route that helps: a **CMHC-insured refinance** that lets you borrow against what your home will be worth once the suite is finished.

## How CMHC Refinance works

Normally, refinancing a home is capped at 80 percent of its current value. Under CMHC’s refinance for secondary suites:

- You can borrow up to **90 percent of the home’s value as improved**, meaning its value with the suite completed.
- The as-improved value must be **under $2 million**.
- Amortization can run up to **30 years**.
- The property can have **up to four units** in total, including the existing ones.
- At least one borrower needs a credit score of **600 or higher**, and the usual debt-service limits apply.
- Funds can be **advanced in stages** as construction progresses.

You need to live in the house. The suite must be **self-contained and legal**, meaning it complies with the building code and zoning, and it **can’t be used as a short-term rental**: no stays shorter than 90 consecutive days.

Mortgage insurance premiums apply at this loan-to-value, and not every lender offers the product. Ask a mortgage broker which lenders do, and what the premium would be on your numbers.

## The $80,000 suite loan you may have heard about

In 2024 the federal government announced the Canada Secondary Suite Loan Program: loans of up to $80,000 at 2 percent over 15 years. It **never opened for applications**, and it was dropped in the 2025 federal budget. Many websites and social media posts still describe it as available. If someone offers to help you apply, be cautious.

## What the lender will want from you

Because the loan is based on the finished value, lenders typically need to see the project clearly before they commit:

1. **Drawings and a building permit**, or proof that one is in process.
2. **A detailed, itemized construction quote.**
3. **An appraisal** of the home as improved.
4. A **draw schedule** that ties each advance to completed work.

This is where the choice of contractor matters. An itemized quote, a realistic schedule and payments tied to stages of work are exactly what we provide on every project, and they’re what lenders ask for.

## Other money that can help

- **Development charges are waived** for second and third units in a house and for one unit in an accessory building. See [adding a second or third unit](/journal/additional-residential-units-york-region).
- If the suite is for a parent aged 65 or older or a family member with a disability, the **Multigenerational Home Renovation Tax Credit** can return up to $7,500. See [renovation rebates for Ontario homeowners](/journal/ontario-home-renovation-rebates).
