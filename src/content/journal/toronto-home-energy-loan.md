The City of Toronto’s Home Energy Loan Program (HELP) lends homeowners **up to $125,000** for energy upgrades at a **fixed rate**, repaid in instalments on the property tax bill over up to 20 years. You hire your own contractor, and the city can advance up to 30 percent of the money before work starts. It pairs well with rebates, because the loan covers the cost upfront while the rebates arrive later.

## What it pays for

Heat pumps, geothermal, insulation (basement, attic and exterior walls), air sealing, window and door replacements, high-efficiency furnaces, boilers, air conditioners and water heaters, rooftop solar panels, battery storage, solar hot water, drain-water heat recovery, low-flow toilets and Level 2 EV chargers.

## Rates and terms

Rates are fixed for the term, valid for loans arranged until **December 31, 2026**:

| Term | Fixed rate |
| --- | --- |
| 5 years | 3.83% |
| 10 years | 4.43% |
| 15 years | 4.85% |
| 20 years | 5.12% |

The 20-year term is available for projects that include solar, windows, geothermal or a heat pump. A 2 percent administrative charge is added, and the total loan can’t exceed $125,000 or 10 percent of your property’s current value assessment, whichever is less. You can pay it off at any time, and the loan is attached to the property rather than to you.

## Who qualifies

- You own a detached, semi, row house, duplex, triplex or other low-rise building of up to three storeys and six units in Toronto (postal code starting with M).
- Every owner on title agrees to take part.
- You haven’t been 60 days late on property tax or utilities more than three times in three years.
- If there’s a mortgage, your lender gives written consent. The city sends you a form for this.

## How it works

1. **Apply online.** The city replies in five to seven business days with a funding offer, once your lender has consented.
2. **Book a home energy assessment** with a registered energy advisor, then get contractor quotes and submit a funding request. Heat pump-only and solar-only projects skip the assessment.
3. **Sign the Property Owner Agreement.** The city can then advance up to 30 percent of the funds.
4. **Complete the work**, have the follow-up assessment done (or, for heat pumps, the installer signs an attestation form), and submit the invoices. The city pays the rest.
5. **Repay** in 11 monthly instalments a year on your property tax bill.

You’re responsible for hiring the contractor and getting any permits, so choose one who itemizes invoices and handles permits as standard.

## Stack it with rebates

HELP can be combined with city, provincial, federal and utility rebates. The [National Heat Pump Rebate](/journal/national-heat-pump-rebate) pays up to $10,000 on heat pumps installed from November 1, 2026, and Ontario’s Home Renovation Savings Program covers insulation, windows and heat pumps; see [renovation rebates for Ontario homeowners](/journal/ontario-home-renovation-rebates).
